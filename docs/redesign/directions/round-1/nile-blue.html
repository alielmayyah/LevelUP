LevelUP visual direction

Nile Blue

EnergeticPreciseUpbeat

Good news should arrive like a bank notification you actually want. A solid cobalt field holds the one answer on every screen, so the sentence reads before any number. Crisp white cards carry the detail. Grey says the machine can’t tell yet, so the member never mistakes a wobble for progress.

Blue is place, never a verdict. The answer field, the wordmark, the active tab, dates. It is never a button and never means better or worse.

Mint/teal is the one accent. The primary action and positive progress, nothing else. Deep teal #0A7766 on white in light; mint #5EE0BE in dark.

Palette

Two separate palettes, each measured in WCAG 2 contrast against the background the color actually sits on. Muscle and fat, the pair that shares a chart, differ in hue (blue against orange, safe for red-green color blindness) and by at least 0.11 in OKLCH lightness in both themes; water sits apart in cyan. No red, violet or indigo anywhere; amber is only a fill under dark text.

Type

Mada in both scripts: one family with a full Arabic design (its Google Fonts Arabic subset ships init, medi, fina, isol and mark positioning), not a Latin face with borrowed Arabic. Every Latin digit is the same width, so numbers are tabular by default. The upright, slightly condensed Arabic keeps a long Egyptian sentence to two or three lines, and the open, sporty Latin keeps pace with it. Weight carries hierarchy, not extra faces. Mada’s Arabic sets smaller than its Latin, so Arabic steps up about 1 px per role, gets about 10% more leading, no tracking, and nothing under 13 px.

Screens

390 × 844 frames, each with its own theme tokens. Each screen puts its answer on the blue field; white cards hold the evidence below. The Home tiles carry the signature: a small noise gauge whose grey zone is the InBody 770’s measurement wobble, which is why weight stays grey while muscle and fat turn mint.

Components

The same parts in both themes. Range bars run like an RTL slider (low on the right); the dot is text-colored so it reads on any band.
